Most teams ask this question at the point where the open floor starts to feel tight — usually
somewhere around four or five people. Here's how to think it through without overspending or
outgrowing your space in three months.

## The short version

- **Coworking desks** suit small, flexible teams that value cost and community over privacy.
- **Private offices** suit teams that take frequent calls, handle confidential work, or have
  outgrown sharing a floor.

The tipping point is usually **privacy and noise**, not headcount.

## Cost: compare per head, not per month

A private office always looks more expensive as a single number. Compare per person instead:

| | Coworking (dedicated desks) | Private office |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Billing | Per person | Per cabin/room |
| Cost for 2–3 people | Usually lower | Usually higher |
| Cost for 6–10 people | Gap narrows | Often competitive |
| Scaling up | Add desks one at a time | Move to a bigger cabin |

For a two-person team, coworking almost always wins on cost. By eight or ten people the
per-head difference is often smaller than expected — and you get a door.

## Where coworking wins

- **Flexibility.** Add or drop desks month to month without renegotiating.
- **Lower commitment.** Shorter notice periods, smaller deposits.
- **Community.** Genuinely useful when you're small — informal introductions, referrals, and
  the simple benefit of not working alone.
- **Cost at small scale.** Hard to beat for one to three people.

## Where a private office wins

- **Calls and meetings.** If your team is on calls most of the day, an open floor becomes a
  daily negotiation over phone booths.
- **Confidentiality.** Client data, hiring conversations, financials — some work shouldn't
  happen at a shared desk.
- **Focus.** A door removes a whole category of interruption.
- **Identity.** Your own space, your setup, your whiteboard left as you left it.

## Questions that decide it

Answer these honestly:

1. **How many hours a day is someone on a call?** More than two or three, and shared space
   starts costing you in productivity.
2. **Would a client overhearing your work be a problem?** If yes, you need a door.
3. **Will your headcount change in the next six months?** Rapid growth favours flexible
   desks until you stabilise.
4. **Does the team come in daily, or a few days a week?** Part-time attendance rarely
   justifies a dedicated cabin.
5. **Do you need to leave equipment set up?** Monitors, test devices, and hardware push you
   toward a private space.

## A middle path worth considering

You don't always have to pick one. Many teams run a hybrid: a small private cabin for the
people who need quiet, plus a few dedicated desks in the open area for the rest. It often
costs less than a cabin big enough for everyone, and you can rebalance as the team changes.

If that sounds right, ask providers whether they'll price a mixed arrangement — many will,
and it rarely appears on the public rate card.

## Deciding

If you're still unsure, the practical test is to visit both in the same afternoon, during
working hours. The difference in noise and focus is immediately obvious in a way no
comparison table conveys.
